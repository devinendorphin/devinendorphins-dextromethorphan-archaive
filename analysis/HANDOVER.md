Generated by `analysis/handover.py`.

**6,944** override events — the model generated, the author rewound past it and typed instead — collapsing to **1,342** distinct after removing duplicated stories. That 5× inflation is the usual trap; all figures use the distinct set.

Overriding the model is not usually a matter of writing more. The author replaces a ~90-word proposal with a handful of words. This is the §2 finding again from the other side: even when the author actively rejects what the model offered, the reply is a cue.

## Casting versus narrating

Share of passages containing a speaker tag (`Name:` at the start of a line) — the corpus's marker for putting someone on stage. Each row is the same set of branch points, so the comparison is paired.

| | passages | contains a speaker tag | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| human override | 359 | **21.7%** | 17.8–26.3% |
| model proposal it replaced | 359 | 23.4% | 19.3–28.0% |

Length is a confound in one direction only — the human passages are far *shorter*, so they have less room to contain a tag. Any excess is therefore conservative.

## Uptake or redirect

**Containment**, not Jaccard: the share of the human override's content words that also appear in the model passage. The two sides differ wildly in length — a handful of words against ~90 — so a symmetric measure would mostly report that asymmetry.

Control: the same human text scored against an abandoned proposal from a **different** branch point in the same story, **length-matched** to within ±30% of the observed proposal's content-word count. Both share the story's cast and vocabulary and are the same size; only the observed pairing is the passage the author had just read and rejected.

| | comparisons | mean content words | mean containment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| observed (the proposal replaced) | 245 | 53.3 | **0.0624** |
| control (elsewhere, length-matched) | 245 | 52.7 | 0.0493 |

Of 245 comparisons, 109 are ties (both zero overlap). Among the 136 that differ, the observed pairing is higher in **63.2%** (95% CI 54.9–70.9%; chance 50%).

**The author carries material forward from the passage they just threw away.** Not much of it — mean containment 0.062 against a 0.049 floor, and nearly half of comparisons show no overlap at all — but the confidence interval clears chance, and it does so after length matching. Rejecting the offer as stated is not the same as discarding it.

Stated plainly: a modest effect on 136 informative comparisons, with the interval's lower bound at 54.9%. An earlier version of this test scored 66.2%, but it compared the *longest* proposal at the branch against a *random* one elsewhere, which inflates containment through length alone. The 63.2% is what survives fixing that.
