## What this settles

**The 2019 break has an explanation, and it is not about text generation.**
`analysis/FACEBOOK.md` records that posting volume jumps an order of magnitude in 2019, a
year before AI Dungeon, and says "whatever changed in 2019 is upstream of everything the
rest of the corpus records". It was: distance from the event, loss of the community, and
the arrival of a thesis.

**The flat eleven years were deliberate.** 2008–2018 averages 20 words a post and never
exceeds 5,000 words a year. That is not a person who wrote little; it is radio silence
held for security, community-safety and conflict-of-interest reasons while running a
growing event. The 2019 jump is the silence ending, not a habit forming.

**Privacy hold confirmed.** The 1,524 message threads stay unread; that work happens
elsewhere. `exports/` remains gitignored and `data/fb_summary.json` stays aggregate-only.

## The videos: he is right about the cohort he is describing, and my question conflated two

I asked about the videos by pointing at the October 2020 posts captioned *"Click for
video: episode 4 —"*. That framing was wrong, and the export says so.

`your_videos.html` holds 512 dated video posts. Their distribution:

```
2011:1  2014:1  2017:1  2018:1  2019:1  2020:16  2021:104  2022:153  2023:132  2024:102
```

The 2020 videos are not spread across the year. Fifteen of the sixteen fall between
2020-10-02 and 2020-11-09 — and then **video posting stops completely for 242 days**, the
largest gap in the entire post-2020 record, resuming 2021-07-09.

That gap is the finding. There are two cohorts, not one:

- **Cohort A, Oct–Nov 2020.** Attached to the numbered episode posts, within seconds:
  episode 3 (+39s), episode 4 (+93s), episode 5 (+27s), episode 6 (+64s). These are the
  ones whose own captions credit *"immersive reader and ai dungeon and open ai and
  microsoft"* and a Creative Commons NIN album.
- **Cohort B, from 2021-07-09.** 104 videos in the back half of 2021 alone.

**VQGAN cannot account for cohort A.** "Taming Transformers for High-Resolution Image
Synthesis" was not posted until December 2020, and the VQGAN+CLIP notebooks that made it a
usable art tool spread in June–July 2021. Cohort B restarts on **2021-07-09**, which is
that arrival almost to the week. So the VQGAN description fits cohort B exactly and cannot
fit cohort A, which predates the paper.

So both things are true: he is right that the videos are VQGAN and pre-broadcast — about
the 104-video cohort — and my question pointed at fifteen earlier videos that are a
different practice with a different toolchain, separated from it by eight months of
silence.

**This matters for `READINGS.md` §IX**, which reads the Love Sharks texts as a score: cues
dictated in, TTS out, nobody in the loop reading. Cohort A is that loop's earliest surviving
output, and the posts describe the toolchain in his own words at the time. It is the
strongest available corroboration of §IX and it is fifteen files, not 1,086.

## Where the strongest idea goes

> authoritarianism is interpersonal violence at scale

This is the load-bearing sentence, and it is not new to the corpus — it is
`READINGS.md` §4/§4b arriving five years earlier, through a different door.

§4b argues that Endorphin's monstrous-hybrid thesis and his small-set-of-shapes thesis are
one thesis: bad faith has few shapes because it optimises under an extra constraint, and
incentive-generated bad faith with no author manufactures the same small set. The route
there was authorless and structural — institutions producing the shapes without anyone
choosing them.

The 2019 sentence runs the implication the other way. Not *the hybrid produces what a
person would produce*, but *the state-level thing is the interpersonal thing, scaled*. And
it is derived from the opposite kind of evidence: not from reading institutions, but from
2016–2019 spent inside an accountability process, a mentor separation, and three years of
bookkeeping against a specific person.

With the correction in place, the derivation is stated outright rather than inferred: it is
**the phenomenology of coercive behaviors** that constitutes "the embryonic beginnings of"
the thesis. The claim is not an analogy noticed between two scales. It is a claim that
studying the mechanism closely at the small scale *is* studying the large one — which is
why the same word, *coercive behaviors*, carries the 2017–2019 paragraph and the 2019 one.

**The push, sharpened by the correction.** The two directions are not equivalent, and the
corpus has been treating them as if they were. A phenomenology is an account of how
something is experienced, and it takes a subject — which is exactly what §4b's authorless
hybrid does not have. So the 2019 route cannot simply be extended to the hybrid case: at
scale there is no one for whom the coercion is a phenomenon in the same way. "Interpersonal violence at scale" claims the mechanism is *the same
mechanism*, larger. §4b's authorless hybrid claims the mechanism is *different* —
incentives, not intent — and converges on the same repertoire. Those make opposite
predictions about attribution, which §X already identified as "the single real boundary":
if authoritarianism is interpersonal violence scaled up, there is always a person at the
end of the bookkeeping, and the thirty-year records exist precisely because he believed
that. If it is the authorless hybrid, the bookkeeping finds no one.

He has held both. The 2019 formulation is the one with a perpetrator in it, and it is the
one he acted on for three years. §4b is the one that lets the perpetrator dissolve. Worth
knowing which he means before §4 and §4b get welded shut as "one thesis" — that welding
may be joining a claim about mechanism to a claim about scale, which is not the same join.

Not tested, and probably not testable against this corpus. Flagged rather than resolved.
