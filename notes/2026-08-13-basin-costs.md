# The cost of each basin

2026-08-13, at Endorphin's request, following
`notes/2026-08-13-enumerating-good-faith.md`. Take each basin of good faith and
each of bad faith; enter from neuroscience; ask what the brain-body has to do to
run it; compute the loss between them; and ask what that says about bad faith
being the easy road — *"the one that takes less energy."*

**Register warning up front.** This is theory, not measurement. Nothing in the
corpus tests it, and nothing below is held to `FINDINGS.md`'s standard. Two
specific hazards are flagged where they bite: **reverse inference** (a region
lighting up in a task does not establish that the region performs the task —
Poldrack's objection, and it invalidates most casual localisation talk), and the
**pathologising trap**, which is the one that matters for this repo and gets its
own section at the end.

---

## The two sets

**Good faith**, six shapes from the enumeration note:

G1 volunteer information that costs you · G2 update under correction · G3 honour
the absent party's interest · G4 mark the limits of your own knowledge · G5 hold
a boundary at your own expense · G6 leave the other's account uncolonised

**Bad faith**, §X's seven collapsed toward five:

B1 control information flow between parties (serial one-on-ones, triangulation) ·
B2 decouple appearance from action (the enthusiastic face) · B3 disable the
witnesses (enmeshment, flying monkeys) · B4 discredit the target's perception
(*crazy*) · B5 claim incapacity (*poor hearing, poor eyesight, poor memory*)

The favourites-and-shit-list item distributes across B1 and B3 rather than
standing alone.

---

## First: the energy premise is false as stated, and the correction is the finding

The naive version of *"takes less energy"* does not survive contact with what is
known about brain metabolism. **The brain's energy budget is overwhelmingly
intrinsic.** It runs at roughly a fifth of basal metabolic rate and stays there;
task-evoked increases above that baseline are on the order of a few per cent.
This is Raichle's result and it is the reason the default-mode network was found
at all. **There is no plausible metabolic accounting on which lying is cheaper
than telling the truth.** The difference would be lost in the noise of being
awake.

Worse for the premise: the deception literature runs the other way. Cognitive-load
approaches to lie detection — Vrij and colleagues — are built on lying being
*more* effortful than truth-telling: you must suppress the true answer, construct
an alternative, monitor its plausibility, and track what you have already said.
Load-inducing interview techniques work *because* deception has less spare
capacity to absorb them.

So the phenomenology is real and the accounting is wrong. **The right currency is
not metabolic energy. It is control.**

The framework that fits is the **expected value of control** account
(Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen): the system treats cognitive control as
intrinsically costly and allocates it only where expected payoff justifies the
outlay. Effort is a real term in the objective function, not an epiphenomenon.
Under EVC, "the easy road" means **the road whose control cost is not charged to
you now** — and that turns out to be a claim about *when* and *to whom* the cost
is billed, not about how much of it there is.

That reframing is what makes the rest of this tractable.

## Good faith, basin by basin

| | what it requires | systems implicated (hedged) | cost profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** volunteer costly info | model the other's epistemic state; inhibit a prepotent self-protective response; accept a foreseen loss | mentalising network (TPJ, mPFC); vmPFC/OFC valuation; dACC conflict monitoring | **acute, one-shot, high.** Peak inhibitory demand at the moment of disclosure; zero afterwards |
| **G2** update under correction | accept prediction error; revise a model; tolerate self-model discrepancy | striatal/dopaminergic prediction error; lateral PFC model revision | **acute, moderate**, plus downstream consistency edits |
| **G3** honour the absent | maintain a representation of an agent who is not present, over delay, with motivational weight and no external cue | mentalising network + hippocampal relational memory + sustained valuation | **sustained, uncued.** The most expensive good-faith basin |
| **G4** mark your limits | estimate own confidence; output the low estimate | anterior/rostrolateral PFC metacognition; insula uncertainty | **low computational, high social** |
| **G5** hold a boundary at cost | maintain a goal against immediate reward; inhibit | dlPFC goal maintenance; IFG inhibition; striatal override | **acute and repeated** |
| **G6** leave the account uncolonised | withhold your own model from expression; suppress narrative completion | inhibitory control + mentalising | **acute; suppression of a default** — we auto-complete other people's stories |

The pattern: good faith's costs are **acute, self-borne, and settled at the point
of action**. G3 is the exception and the reason it feels like the hardest one.

## Bad faith, basin by basin

| | what it requires | systems implicated (hedged) | cost profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** control information flow | maintain *N* audience-models; track what each knows; keep them consistent | recursive mentalising; working memory; episodic **source** memory | **scales with N**, deferred, maintenance-heavy |
| **B2** appearance ≠ action | hold a dual representation, displayed vs actual; regulate expression | mentalising + expression regulation + inhibition | **low per episode, high cumulative** |
| **B3** disable the witnesses | model third parties' relations *to each other*; second-order mentalising | recursive mentalising; social network representation | **high setup, then near-zero.** Amortises |
| **B4** discredit perception | deliver sustained inconsistency credibly | expression regulation; low novel demand once running | **near-zero to run** — the target performs the reconciliation |
| **B5** claim incapacity | withhold performance of a capacity | almost nothing | **the cheapest move in either taxonomy** |

## The loss

Write both as objectives and the asymmetry falls out.

**Bad faith** minimises immediate cost to self, subject to an appearance
constraint:

> minimise `cost_to_self(t)` subject to `appears_cooperative(t)`

**Good faith** minimises divergence between what is represented and what is
communicated, with no cost constraint at all:

> minimise `| represented_state − communicated_state |`

Three differences follow, and only the third is the one he is pointing at.

**1. Bad faith carries a discount rate; good faith does not.** B1–B4 are all
cheap *now* and expensive *later* — the ledger of who-was-told-what accrues, and
B1's cost grows with the number of audiences. Good faith's basins charge at the
moment of action and then stop. **"The easy road" is a statement about temporal
discounting, not about total cost.** Integrated over a relationship, bad faith is
almost certainly the more expensive path. Integrated over the next five minutes,
it is not — and five minutes is the window the choice is made in.

**2. The maintenance cost is externalised.** This is the sharpest term and it is
where the corpus meets the theory. B4 is not cheap because discrediting is easy;
it is cheap **because the target does the work.** The person being told that
their perception is unreliable is the one who has to reconcile the inconsistency,
re-examine memory, and rebuild the account. The 05-30 triangulation post is a
description of exactly this: the mechanism wears the Other down until they
produce *"the anxious and volatile behaviors that the Person had been telling the
Third about the whole time."* **The books get kept. They are kept by the other
person.**

Which makes one thing about this repo hard to unsee. His 2017–2019 *"bookkeeping
to solidify the records enough to where they could be evidence"* is the
externalised ledger being maintained by the party it was pushed onto. The
archive is the invoice.

**3. Only bad faith has a basin whose cost goes to zero.** B3 amortises — once a
network is enmeshed it maintains itself without further input — and B5 costs
essentially nothing at any point. **No good-faith basin has this property.** G1
through G6 all charge every time they are run; none of them can be set up once
and left running. That is a real structural asymmetry and it is the strongest
version of his intuition: not that bad faith is cheaper per move, but that **bad
faith contains fixed-cost moves and good faith is all marginal cost.**

A note on B5 worth flagging: claimed incapacity is the cheapest item in either
list, and it is **absent from the 2019 enumeration and present in 2023**. If the
basins are ordered by cost, the cheapest one appearing last is consistent with it
being a late or terminal strategy — what is left when the more expensive
performances can no longer be sustained. Untested, and the sample is two lists.

## On "differently shaped structures" — where I will not follow, and why

The question asks what this says about how the structures might be *shaped
differently* to make the bad-faith basin the easy road. There is literature that
appears to answer this: paralimbic and amygdala differences associated with
psychopathy (Kiehl), and acquired sociopathy following ventromedial prefrontal
damage (Damasio, Anderson and colleagues) — the latter being the cleaner result,
since the lesion precedes the behaviour and the causal direction is not in doubt.

I do not think it should be used here, for three reasons.

**It does not cover the phenomenon.** Bad faith as enumerated is something most
people do situationally, under pressure, in ordinary institutions. The structural
literature concerns a small, extreme population. Explaining B1–B5 by paralimbic
volume is explaining the common case by the rare one.

**Group differences do not license individual inference.** These are modest
effects with wide overlap between distributions. Nothing about them supports
looking at a person and reading their basin off their anatomy.

**And it walks straight back into the thing he refused.** The black bars in the
July series exist because *"when a human construct like that is pathologized it
can also be stigmatized"* — the whole operation was built to run without the
diagnostic category. **A structural account reintroduces the category through the
back door with a biological warrant, which is harder to refuse than a DSM label,
not easier.** *"Their brains are shaped differently"* is the same claim as the
one he struck out, wearing better credentials. The basin framing was attractive
precisely because a basin is where a trajectory lands rather than what someone
is; grounding the basin in anatomy converts it back into what someone is.

**The available alternative keeps the explanatory work and drops the essentialism:
depth of a basin is a function of reinforcement history, not of structure.** An
attractor gets deep because trajectories have repeatedly landed there and been
rewarded — which is learning, is developmental, is contingent, and is in
principle reversible. That fits his own 2019 aetiology (*"THEIR wounds, inflicted
before personhood began"*) without requiring the further claim that the wound
left a differently shaped organ. It also fits *"disorders are just different
Orders"*, which anatomy does not.

## What would test any of this

Nothing here is measured, and most of it is not measurable from this corpus. Two
things could be:

- **The externalisation claim** predicts an asymmetry in *who writes the ledger*.
  Across the archives, the person maintaining the detailed record of an
  interaction should be the one on the receiving end of B1/B4. The corpus is one
  data point and it is his own; the AI Dungeon and NovelAI sides contain
  scenarios with both roles and could be examined for whether the record-keeping
  falls to the same position.
- **The fixed-cost claim** predicts that B3 and B5 persist in accounts of
  long-running situations while B1 and B2 drop out as the audience count grows.
  The 2019-versus-2023 enumerations are two samples and already gesture that way
  — B5 appears only in the later one. More enumerations, or the second Facebook
  account, would make that more than a gesture.
