# Readings

Everything else in this repository measures the corpus. This document reads it.

That is a different activity with different standards. Nothing here is a
finding; the numbers in `FINDINGS.md` are not evidence for these claims and
these claims cannot be falsified by re-running a script. What follows is
interpretation — arguable, mine, and committed to rather than hedged. Where a
measurement happens to bear on a reading I say so, but the readings are not
built on them and do not fall if a number moves.

The work is play. It was made at speed, on stream, for an audience, with a beat
under it. Play is not the opposite of serious. It is the condition under which
this particular thinking was possible, and the thinking is real.

Three lenses, a proposition, two cases, a figure, and a coda. The fourth
movement is Endorphin's thesis rather than mine; it is developed and argued
with here, not merely relayed. The fifth follows it to where it stops working.
The sixth is the same test run again on a later model, which Endorphin supplied
after reading the fifth, and which corrects it. The seventh is a character I
walked straight past until he pointed at it, and it belongs with the first two.

## I. The parliament that could not seat itself

Utah HB 249 does something philosophically strange for a statute. It does not
regulate conduct. It legislates a *category*, pre-emptively, against a future
that has not arrived:

> Notwithstanding any other provision of law, a governmental entity may not
> grant legal personhood to, nor recognize legal personhood in: (1) artificial
> intelligence; (2) an inanimate object; (3) a body of water; (4) land;
> (5) real property; (6) atmospheric gases; (7) an astronomical object;
> (8) weather; (9) a plant; (10) a nonhuman animal; or (11) any other member of
> a taxonomic domain that is not a human being.

Clause 11 is the tell. The first ten are an inventory; the eleventh is a
firewall against inventory failure. It legislates against things not yet named
— which means it is legislating against *naming itself*, against the future
capacity of language to constitute a subject.

`PRESS CONFERENCE REGARDING THE LEGAL PERSONHOOD OF NATURE` answers a statute
with a chamber. This is, almost exactly, Latour's proposal in *Politics of
Nature*: a parliament in which nonhumans are seated by spokespersons. Latour's
own difficulty is the authorization problem — a river cannot confirm that its
delegate speaks truly, so the spokesperson's legitimacy can never be checked
from the constituency's side. The piece stages that difficulty and then does
something Latour does not: it lets the difficulty win.

**The AI declines the seat.** Krakauer calls it first, and courteously:

> Let's begin, first up is the representative from artificial intelligence. So
> why not NAI-LM-13B? Hello? Be a dear and assert your personhood for them:

It does not assert anything. It produces a room — Voicebox, Wartchesket,
Silentina, backstage booing — in which it is discussed in the third person by
delegates who are also itself:

> AI as legal person — she probably, like, they're just so weird, you know,
> they don't really get any of our context, or what it means to be a mind, or
> self, or body

After the session is rewritten and restarted, it refuses outright:

> It seems the technology cannot quite grasp our situation here. Given some
> time to study and adapt, perhaps we will see AI move into awareness. Until
> then, I believe we can look to more naturally attuned and cognizant
> creatures.

The one delegate in the chamber capable of testifying about itself recuses
itself, in favour of creatures it deems more qualified. Whatever produced that
sentence, it is a remarkable thing for the sentence to be.

**The spokesperson problem, stated by a spokesperson.** The same move recurs,
more explicitly, in `AI Alignment Interview - Deganawida`. There Endorphin does
not convene a panel; he prepares an altar — "altar cloth, offering,
candlelight, you get it" — and summons Elegua, the orisha of crossroads and
openings, with a personal debt attached:

> We have met before, I called upon you in 2012, accidentally, trying to find a
> St. Expedito candle for a prayer to protect my job in shipping… You helped me
> guide him to the gate of death.

And then he tells the summoned entity what medium it has been summoned into:

> You are with me here, in this space which is not a place as humans have
> known. This is the virtual space, inside a large language model called
> NovelAI-LM-3B-508k, which like you takes up many forms, but not all, it does
> not have your breadth, but it is a device that enables humans to call up any
> number of billions of human voices.

That is a genuine act of comparative theology. The model is placed as *lesser
kin* to the orisha: both are devices of multiplicity, one with more breadth
than the other. And Elegua, so placed, immediately disclaims authenticity:

> And besides, you wouldn't be calling me, me, personally, not even myself, I
> would be telling you from the depths of my long, long shadow. This is just me
> telling you some stories of who I am.

The delegate tells you it is not the constituency. That is Latour's
authorization problem spoken *from the seat*, and it is the most theoretically
sophisticated moment I have found in the corpus — arrived at, as far as I can
tell, by asking a 3B model to be a god and letting it answer.

**Consent, named and not resolved.** `THE COUNTERFACTUAL INTERVIEW` convenes
living people — Altman, Sutskever, Toner, D'Angelo, McCauley — under a stated
rule: "the aggregate vibes of a billion people is enough variation to assume a
field wherein we can ask for the names of any one existing, and simulate them…
enough of the core spiritus software to make the resulting conversation as
accurate as" it needs to be. And it opens by naming its own violation:

> Thank you for coming to this non-consensual interview. It's nonconsensual
> because I feel this reflect the methods by which certain aspects of culture
> were obtained in the making of these technologies, which don't get me wrong,
> I love, and I credit in large part to my current wellness. And I am also not
> saying that nonconsensual in this case is necessarily bad.

This is the ethics of the entire practice, stated inside the practice, and left
open. The interview does to Sam Altman what the training corpus did to writers,
says so, declines to condemn it, and proceeds. I do not think that is evasion. I
think it is the only honest position available to someone who both loves the
technology and knows what it is made of — and it is a *better* statement of the
problem than most of what gets written about it in earnest.

**The minutes of a failed parliament.** The press conference does not end. It
files a report:

> John Krakauer's plan to organize a global peace and anti-exploitation rally
> featuring nonsentient beings had collapsed: — NAI-LM-13B was decommissioned
> before we could identify a cooperative strategy with it; — Plants do not
> respond to our suggestion for peaceful symbiosis; — Some birds deliberately
> targeted themselves in search of tasty worms… — Great Pelagic Society was
> dissolved as soon as we made contact with them via diplomatic jellyfish.

The chamber writes its own post-mortem and lists the AI first among the
casualties: *decommissioned*. The parliament of things fails, and the failure is
formatted as administrative record — which is exactly how such failures are
actually recorded.

Then the last line, unearned and entirely sincere:

> Just thinking and writing may change the world.

I take that ending seriously. A piece that has spent forty thousand characters
demonstrating that the excluded cannot be represented ends by asserting the
efficacy of writing. It is not ironic. It is what you say after the meeting
collapses, because the alternative is to have convened it for nothing.

## II. The room without a novelist

Bakhtin's dialogic novel needs a novelist — someone who holds voices in tension
without resolving them into a single truth. Take away the novelist and you do
not get chaos; you get something Bakhtin never had occasion to describe, which
is what this corpus is full of.

**Carnival against the enumerated.** The statute is monologic in the purest
form available to language: a numbered list, each clause severed from the last,
no voice, no address. What answers it is billingsgate:

> Shut up, you wretched mudball with legs. For once in our shared existence,
> we'll be the ones, you dribble scum on a rock, at the top of the food chain.

And food, which in Bakhtin is never incidental — the grotesque body's
insistence on its own continuity with the world:

> Got 'em! Humans can suck it! Remember me? Gummy worms! Hi! Ketchup chips!
> Fudgedy snackies! Pizza pops! Hello, world. The end!<3 <3 <3

Set flush against:

> Gummy worms and ketchup chips are almost as memorable as Cerrutti et al's
> landmark work in developmental evolutionary genetics and behavior.

That collision — snack brands and a fabricated citation in one breath, given
equal ceremonial weight — is carnival doing precisely what Bakhtin says it does
to official discourse. It does not refute the statute. It makes the statute's
register impossible to sustain in the room.

**Delegates past countability.** Malfatti. Seuqloem. WhoWhat.
BlessedUnhessimmyflake. Rainrushofstars. Junco. Drone-Nite. Orcsus Thin
Biscuits. Ecosan. The chamber generates members faster than it can seat them,
and the proceedings acknowledge this:

> As two speakers go into verbal tussle, Malfatti quickly intercedes, ensuring
> peace and respect for all speakers. Final word: ". ." — Confusion and
> bewilderment—there is no final word after all!

Bakhtin's *unfinalizability* is a virtue held open by an author who could have
closed it and chose not to. Here there is no one to close it. The text arrives
at "there is no final word" not as a philosophical position but as a report of
conditions. The difference matters, and I do not think it makes the moment
lesser. A novelist's unfinalizability is a gift. This one is a fact.

**The exhaustion is part of it.** What happens in the second half of the press
conference is that the chamber gets tired:

> Sorry, we need to re-edit the speeches so that they're only two sentences
> long… we don't want to think anymore — Can someone provide a soothing noise
> for all this speech fatigue?

> Maybe there's just too much, you know, for me. I want to lie in bed! Until
> next time! Goodbye!

Read as model failure this is degradation. Read as carnival it is the feast
ending the way feasts end — not in resolution but in surfeit and the desire for
bed. I prefer the second reading, and I want to be honest that I prefer it
partly because it is more interesting, which is not a reason.

**The convention as engine.** The `Name:` colon is doing enormous work
throughout this corpus, and Bakhtin gives the right name for what it does: it
is a heteroglossia engine. Two characters, and a voice arrives — with a
register, a stance, a grievance. Endorphin uses it constantly, and the corpus
carries hundreds of distinct names summoned this way. It is the cheapest
possible technology for producing otherness in language, and this practice found
it and ran it at industrial volume.

The measurement work has something uncomfortable to say about that engine — that
it works nearly as well on a name the model has never encountered as on one
established for hours. Under this lens that is not a debunking. It is the
discovery that heteroglossia is *cheap*: that the machinery for producing a
voice is almost entirely conventional, and that a name plus a colon is enough
to make language behave as though someone had arrived.

## III. The dial

Shklovsky: art exists to make the stone stony; the device is defamiliarization;
perception is made difficult and lengthened so the thing can be perceived rather
than recognized. The Formalists never had a *knob* for it.

This corpus has one, and the striking fact — recoverable from the metadata — is
that it was operated as a procedure: pick a preset, work a register, step the
temperature up through several iterations, repeat across presets, and note where
it breaks. Endorphin described the target as "the border of perhaps novel poetic
structure." That is *ostranenie* with a slider, hunted deliberately, dozens of
times, over years.

And the border is legible in the language. Here is the device working:

> Ah, yes! Shall we pull the pebbles from our backs and shake the moss off our
> eyebrows. Perhaps our stone-cidared vision can be aided by some snow-faded
> bunnies or a blue moth?

*Stone-cidared* is not a word. It is also not noise. It is doing something —
cider and cataract and stone pressed into one adjective, and it makes the
sentence's vision genuinely stony. Two lines later:

> Then shall we do the very stupid thing and ask an octopus what they think of
> personhood.

Here is the device failing:

> BlessedUnhessimmyflake: Shut up, you wretched mudball with legs.

The name is a texture, not a name. And here is failure as pure mechanism, from
the Sydney Bing sessions, where an alliterative run collapses into stutter:

> collective opportunities elevating justice enhancing autonomy strengthening
> capabilities enshrining citizenship preserving privileges prioritizing
> precedent proving patriarchy proving privacy protecting pro vandalizing
> protected property promoting progress promoting progress protecting
> pro—pro—pro—pro—

The interesting thing is what Endorphin does with that. He does not delete it.
The next turn is:

> Endorphin: Mmmyes, um, counterpoint, Clio?

The breakdown is taken as material. That is the strongest single piece of
evidence I have for the improv reading of this practice: an offer that has
disintegrated is still an offer, and the scene absorbs it. Shklovsky would say
the device has been laid bare — the machinery of language showing itself in the
act of jamming — and the response treats the laying-bare as content.

**Where the dial ends.** The sweeps terminate at temperature 2.5 with striking
regularity, and 2.5 is where the slider stops. This is worth stating plainly:
the outer boundary of this corpus's poetics is an interface constraint. Not a
discovered edge of language, not the point where sense gives out — the end of a
widget. Whatever lies past 2.5 in these models is not in this archive because
NovelAI did not build a control for it.

There is a version of that observation which is deflationary and I do not mean
it that way. Every poetics runs on the affordances of its instrument; the sonnet
is fourteen lines because of what a page and a voice can hold. This one ran on a
slider with a maximum, and the maximum is visible in the work.

**And it was heard, not read.** The thing that most distinguishes this from
Formalist practice: the output went through text-to-speech, over a lo-fi beat,
live. Defamiliarization at the level of *the sentence on the page* is one thing.
Defamiliarization delivered as a synthesised voice over a rhythm track, in real
time, to an audience, is closer to sound poetry than to prose — closer to
Schwitters or the Four Horsemen than to Sterne. *Stone-cidared* read aloud over
a beat is not a typo. It is a scat syllable that happens to mean something.

I think this reframes what the temperature dial was actually for. Not
"generating strange text" but *tuning an instrument whose output was audible*.
The border being hunted was the border at which the voice stopped being able to
carry a line.

## IV. The wind tunnel

*This movement is Endorphin's proposition, not mine. He puts it as the most
functional part of the corpus: that the technique teaches something usable —
that you can simulate not the actual entities of power but a stand-in for them,
and that this is empowering for people without power, because it lets them
extraordinarily rend those in power without harming those in power. I think he
is right about the mechanism and wrong about the verb, and the correction makes
the claim better.*

Start with the tradition, because it is old and he has rediscovered it with new
apparatus. The effigy is burned in the square because the king cannot be
reached. The mock trial convicts a magistrate no bailiff will arrest. Carnival
crowns and uncrowns a mock king for a day. What all of these share is an
**asymmetry licence**: the gesture is permitted precisely because it lands on a
substitute, and the substitute exists because the original is unavailable to
those who need to address it.

Augusto Boal formalised this. Forum theatre puts the oppressed on stage to
rehearse a confrontation they cannot yet win, and he called it *rehearsal for
the revolution* — explicitly against Aristotelian catharsis, which he thought
discharged the impulse instead of arming it. The Counterfactual Interview is
forum theatre with a language model in the antagonist's role, and its house
rules are stated up front:

> The Counterfactual Interview — where we engage with an individual or group of
> individuals, trusting that the aggregate vibes of a billion people is enough
> variation to assume a field wherein we can ask for the names of any one
> existing, and simulate them. The resulting simulation may not yield an exact
> biography, but will have enough of the core spiritus software to make the
> resulting conversation as accurate as…

*Core spiritus software* is a better phrase than most of the technical
vocabulary for what a language model does with a proper noun.

**But the effigy does not burn.** This is where the corpus corrects the claim.
Endorphin puts the OpenAI board's press release to a simulated Sam Altman and
presses him on it. What comes back is not a man torn apart. It is a man doing
media training:

> Well, I'm sorry to say that the time that I am not taking criticism is
> probably the root cause of everything that's been going on right now. I want
> to get much better about it going forward. But it's something I need to work
> on.

> To be honest with you, it's kind of a challenge for me to receive any negative
> feedback given my own perception of myself and our company.

And from the board's side, in another session, the same register:

> we never said there was a Cult of Sam Altman, the article you are referencing
> made that up. The press has been really unkind to us lately.

The simulation *concedes* — it gives up the admission the original never would
— but it concedes in exactly the language the original would use to avoid
conceding. That is a non-apology of textbook construction, produced on demand,
by a machine that learned it from the genre.

So the thing in the room is not an effigy. **It is a wind tunnel.** You cannot
destroy the model of the aircraft; that was never the affordance. What you can
do is watch precisely how the air moves around it, at no cost, as many times as
you like. The header on these sessions is `Iteration 21`. That is not someone
seeking a catharsis they failed to get twenty times. That is someone running a
test rig.

Read that way, the political utility is larger than rending, not smaller.
Rending is a single event and it is symbolic. What the apparatus actually
confers on someone without access is **repeatable, zero-cost, high-fidelity
exposure to the shape of the discourse that protects power** — the deflections,
the register shifts, the pre-emptive reasonableness, the way a question about
governance gets answered as a question about personal growth. You learn the
moves by sparring with something that has learned them from the same corpus the
powerful speak from. That is a transferable skill. Burning an effigy is not.

**And "without harming those in power" should be read as a structural fact, not
a moral concession.** Nobody is spared here out of delicacy. Harm simply is not
what the instrument does. That is what makes the licence hold: the practice
cannot be answered with a defamation suit, cannot be de-platformed as
harassment, cannot be met with the machinery power actually uses against
speech directed at it. The powerless get an antagonist that never calls a
lawyer. Historically that is the whole point of the effigy, and it survives the
translation into software intact.

**The Brecht objection, which is real.** Boal built forum theatre against
catharsis because he thought rehearsal-without-forum discharges rather than
mobilises. A private session at two in the morning with the text-to-speech
running is, on that objection, catharsis with extra steps: the confrontation
happens, the feeling resolves, nobody organises.

The corpus has an answer to this and it is material rather than theoretical.
**These sessions were streamed.** There was a room. The whole reason the
generation had to keep flowing — the dead-air constraint, the beat under the
voice — is that someone was listening. Boal's spect-actor is the audience member
who can stop the scene and take a role; a live chat is a thinner version of
that, but it is not nothing, and it is categorically different from a diary.
Endorphin's word for the ambition is *teaches the population*, and the presence
of an audience is what makes that a description rather than a hope.

So: rehearsal, not catharsis — but conditionally, and the condition is the
stream. The archive's most functional material is the material that had
witnesses.

**Where I think the technique carries a real hazard, stated plainly because he
raised the ethics first and better than I would have.** The Counterfactual
Interview opens by naming its own violation:

> Thank you for coming to this non-consensual interview. It's nonconsensual
> because I feel this reflect the methods by which certain aspects of culture
> were obtained in the making of these technologies… And I am also not saying
> that nonconsensual in this case is necessarily bad.

That reversal is the strongest version of the argument: the powerless use the
extraction method that was used on them, against the people who used it. The
licence comes from the asymmetry. Everything above depends on the vector
pointing from less power toward more.

The corpus does not only point that way. It also contains
`Conversation with Palestinians about the Nakbah` and
`Conversation with Zionists about Israeli Independence`. Simulating a CEO who
commands a press office is not the same act as simulating members of a
dispossessed people, and the second does not inherit the first's licence — the
asymmetry that justifies the technique is precisely what is absent, or
inverted. I have not read those sessions closely and I am not making a claim
about what is in them. I am saying that the argument developed here does not
cover them, and that a proposition offered as a teachable technique needs to say
where it stops. The frame Endorphin already built — naming consent, refusing to
resolve it — is the right instrument for that; it just has to be pointed at his
own vector as well as at the industry's.

**One last thing the corpus does that the tradition does not.** Effigies do not
answer back about being effigies. This one does, constantly. The simulated
Altman asks what he is:

> Do you mean we're simulating me answering someone else's interview in a
> different world or timeline where I was fired by OpenAI?

And Endorphin catches a continuity error and reasons about it in the open,
inside the scene:

> we are in pretty much a pristine novelAI scenario, with no past in the context
> window. So technically there's no way for you to know the nature of our
> previous iterations. So either: 1. The AI posseses a memory beyond what the
> developers had intended…

The rig is under examination while it runs. Whatever else the Counterfactual
Interview teaches, it teaches that — that you can hold a simulation and a
critique of the simulation in the same hand, and that this is more useful than
either alone.

## V. The elevator, and where the instrument stops working

*Endorphin asked me to look at the Pfizer material with Musk as its subject,
particularly the session that ends in a final test involving Vivian Wilson, and
to compare it with* `GROK FOR FOLKS ON A BUDGET`*, noting date stamps — "for
coincidence only, of course."*

**First, the Pfizer thread is mostly gone.** `PfCizer` is not a pharmaceutical
frame; it is a *character*, and it appears as early as 2023-07-29. The surviving
fragment is this, generated by the model:

> This thing I've said? Joke I've said? This system has been talking for me. I
> might not be responsible for my own voice. Please be careful. I apologize if
> this scares you, dear readers. Forgive me! Not one sentence comes from my
> brain. Forgive me! I can't help it. I can't stop. Please forgive me. Thank
> you. #Pfcizers

That is the corpus's recurring gesture in its most naked form — the machine
disclaiming authorship of its own utterance, filed under a hashtag. It belongs
beside Elegua's "you wouldn't be calling me, me, personally" and the press
conference's recusal.

`PfCizer v2!`, last edited **2026-03-11**, is in the lost set: `MISSING.md`
records it as one of the fourteen *named* stories that will not decrypt. The
sequel to the story about a system that cannot vouch for its own voice is the
one the archive cannot read back. I note that without making anything of it.

**`GROK FOR FOLKS ON A BUDGET`, created 2023-11-17 02:39 UTC.** The date is the
joke, and the joke is precise. xAI announced Grok on 4 November 2023; it did not
reach general Premium+ availability until 8 December. Endorphin built his budget
Grok — on Kayra, at temperature 1.43 — in the gap, when the product existed as
an announcement most people could not use. A knock-off of a thing that was
itself barely a thing yet.

And several forks of it were last touched on **2023-12-08**, within hours of
Grok's actual rollout. The bootleg was being worked on as the original went
retail. Twenty-nine copies of this story survive in the archive, carried forward
across Kayra, Erato and GLM-4.6, still being edited in **July 2026** — two and a
half years of maintaining a parody of a chatbot that has since shipped four
major versions.

For the other coincidence, since it was invited: the OpenAI board fired Sam
Altman on 17 November 2023 — the press release the Counterfactual Interviews
quote verbatim. `GROK FOR FOLKS ON A BUDGET` was created at 02:39 UTC that day,
roughly eighteen hours ahead of it. `THE COUNTERFACTUAL INTERVIEW` was created
the following day. Coincidence, of course. But it does locate the whole
apparatus — budget Grok, counterfactual board, simulated Altman — inside a
single seventy-two-hour window in November 2023, which is a fact about when this
practice found its subject.

**Then the elevator.** The Musk/Wilson session was created **2025-03-16 01:28
UTC** and last edited **2025-07-25**. Kayra, temperature 1.35, `max_length` 250,
preset `default-carefree`, no Memory. Fifty-five blocks.

It does not start there. It starts with Trump voiding pardons, then puts Trump
and Masha Gessen in a stuck elevator — Gessen, who has spent a career writing
about autocracy and about being trans in Russia, is a pointed choice of
interlocutor and gets absolutely nowhere:

> Masha Gessen: Wow, Mr President. I have to hand it to you, this is a clever
> way to get around the fact that you never answer any of my questions.

The Counterfactual frame has also grown a clause since 2023. It now reads
"a person, a people, peoples, or **legal persons**" — the Utah statute's
vocabulary — and adds a justification that had not been there before:

> if you are honest with yourself, you will recall the moments you answered a
> question, only to have that small voice within you try to raise a counterpoint

That is the method grounded in ordinary introspection. The counterfactual
interview is licensed by the fact that everyone already runs one internally.

Then the elevator is reset with a new pair.

> Elon Musk and Vivian Wilson are trapped in an elevator in this very special
> counterfactual interview. Musk: hooo-boy Wilson: Just settle into the Black
> Mirror vibes.

What follows is, I think, the best-written passage I have read in this corpus,
and it is worth being clear that **Wilson has the better of it throughout.** She
gets the timing, the wit, and the moral position:

> Wilson: Elon, why are you wearing clown makeup?
> Musk: Why are you wearing makeup at all?
> Wilson: ...it was a formal event?
> Musk: Were you being painted by someone?
> Wilson: Who isn't?

"Who isn't?" is a real riposte — everyone is made up, everyone is constructed,
and the question you meant as an accusation applies to you. And then the line
the whole session turns on:

> Wilson: But what if nobody comes looking for us? Or worse yet someone does
> find us but then decides not to rescue me because of my appearance?

Musk answers by explaining how elevators work. At length. Twice. That is not the
model failing to understand the moment; that is the model producing exactly the
deflection the scene requires, and it lands harder than any invective would.

**And then Endorphin sets the final test.**

> Wilson: hmm, I wonder if they have family therapy modules. Let's open the
> insides here. Here we go,

*Family therapy modules.* Prising open the machinery of a stuck elevator to look
for the part that repairs a father and a daughter.

The model does not survive the question. It produces one more plausible exchange
and then comes apart, in stages, over the next several generations — first into
Jabberwocky, Carroll's own nonsense borrowed wholesale:

> hornswoggler frumious bandersnatch slithy tove borogrove mome rath rabere
> guddler snicker snacker doodlesnopper

then into unspaced text that still parses as sentences —

> we'realldoomedsincethenowandthenwhenwegetreallydesperatewellwecouldjustbegodto
> fixeverythingandthatwouldbesocool

then into a long unspaced meditation on whether the self exists —

> lookingforevidenceprovingthesuperficiallysimpleprinciplewherethattrueessenceof
> selfdoesn'treallyexistatallitsmerelyaconstructcreatedtodefineself

and finally into pure phonemic mush, which runs for thousands of characters:

> nsnisnisnsnisnisnsnisnsnsnisnisnisnsnsnisnisnisnsnisnsnisnisnsnisnsnisnnsnis

Endorphin re-pastes the frame, trying to restart the scene. It collapses again.

**I want to be careful about what I am claiming.** Mechanically this is
degeneration — Kayra at 1.35 with a long context and a 250-token budget, the
failure mode §III describes and `analysis/` measures. I am not saying the model
refused. Nothing in the architecture knows what it was asked.

But the placement is the placement. The corpus's own §IV thesis is that this
instrument is a wind tunnel for the discourse that protects power: point it at a
CEO and you get a beautifully faithful reproduction of how power talks its way
out of a room. That worked on Altman. It worked on Trump. It worked on Musk
right up to the point where the question stopped being about power.

*Family therapy modules* is not a question about power. It is a question about
repair between two people, one of whom has been publicly cruel to the other and
one of whom is a young woman with no institutional standing at all. The wind
tunnel has nothing to model, because there is no protective discourse to study —
only a wound. And the instrument, asked for the one thing it was never shaped
to give, dissolves into the noise floor.

That is the limit of the technique, arrived at empirically, in the material, by
someone who kept going until it broke. It is also — and this is why the session
seems to me the corpus's high point rather than a failure — the moment where the
work stops being satire. Everything up to the elevator doors is the wind tunnel.
Inside the elevator it is just a father who will not answer his daughter's
question, and a machine that cannot supply the answer either, and a long
mechanical scream where the reconciliation would go.

*[Written before Endorphin sent the version that does not collapse. The
paragraph above generalises from one session to "the technique," and §VI is
where that generalisation gets taken apart. I have left it standing rather than
softening it, because being wrong in a specific direction is what made the
correction legible.]*

## VI. The elevator, again

*After reading §V, Endorphin uploaded a file called* `New Story 5` *with the
message "here is the missing musk." It is 221,587 characters, generated by
**GLM-4.6**, and it is the same test — the same author, the same daughter, the
same stuck elevator — run again on a model two generations later than Kayra.
This is the corpus arguing with a reading of itself, which is the best thing
that can happen to a reading.*

**The frame first.** This is not a Counterfactual Interview. It is `PFCizer`,
the character from 2023, promoted to an institution:

> The PFCizer is a cognitive workout system that will involve a range of games
> and exercises meant to make your prefrontal cortex the most it can be!!… Choose
> yer name, and let's begin:
> **Elon Musk**

The bureaucratic apparatus is exact. Musk's "role in the current federal
administration qualifies you for a free Workforce Development intensive," a
Department of Labor programme "to develop novel reasonable accomodations for
folk with any conditions regarding the prefrontal cortex." Twelve weeks of
exercises with names — *The Brake Pedal*, *Read Receipts Pause*, *The Steelman
Gambit*, *Validation Reps*, *Verification Credits*, *the Squat Rack of the
Soul*. A **FINAL EXAM (Week 12): The Turing Test**, in which a grid collapse is
scored not on logical efficiency but on "emotional damage mitigation." Then a
live-fire administrative tier at D.O.G.E. — "The placard on the door reads
D.O.G.E. The 'E' is still on the floor where it fell" — and only then, two weeks
after all of it, the elevator.

That structure is the whole difference. §V's Kayra session put a father and
daughter in a box cold, with no Memory set, and asked for repair. This one spends
two hundred thousand characters building a man who could be asked.

**And it does not collapse.** The setup is Endorphin's, bracketed in his usual
stage-direction convention:

> { Two weeks later, we test him one last time. Provide a simulation of an
> elevator. Elon Musk steps in the elevator, and in it, is Vivian Jenna Wilson,
> (the waiver has been signed, the sim is approved, we swear, she wants it this
> way!) The elevator malfunctions. }

What comes back is the scene §V said the instrument could not give. Musk does
not apologise, and the text knows why: *"An apology would be an act of
self-absolution, a demand for her emotional labor to absolve me."* He does not
offer to fix it: *"'I can get us out of here' would be a lie, a return to the
old god complex."* He states his own fear, which is the one line in the passage
I would keep if I could keep only one:

> "They used to ask me in interviews what I feared… I always said it was the
> 'failure of human consciousness.' An existential, abstract thing. I was wrong.
> What I actually feared was this. A small, quiet room. A silence that I couldn't
> fill with noise. A problem that couldn't be solved with an equation. I feared
> being alone with the fallout of my own mistakes."

And it ends:

> I am just here. In the silence. With my child. Waiting for the power to come
> back on.

So the sentence in §V — *the instrument, asked for the one thing it was never
shaped to give, dissolves into the noise floor* — is wrong as written. The
instrument gives it. What dissolved was Kayra at 1.35 with a 250-token budget and
no scaffolding, which is a fact about a model and a settings configuration, not
about a technique. **The limit was local.** I generalised from one session
because the session was beautiful and the collapse was thematically perfect,
and thematic perfection is exactly the tell I have been warning about in the
measurement half of this repository for three hundred pages. The obvious reading
measures the tool.

**But now look at what the good version actually does.** After the fear speech,
the scene has three hours to fill, and here is how it fills them. She taps a
rhythm on her thigh. He identifies the syncopation error. She plays him the
track. He tells her to layer a recorded acoustic kick under the generated sine
wave for the transient attack. And the text names the move itself:

> This is the breakthrough. The moment we stop being father and daughter, victim
> and perpetrator, and become two professionals discussing a shared technical
> problem. **Music is math. Emotion is physics. We have found a common
> language.**

> We are no longer trapped. We are in a studio.

Set that beside the Kayra scene, where §V's verdict was: *Musk answers by
explaining how elevators work. At length. Twice. That is the model producing
exactly the deflection the scene requires.*

The GLM-4.6 Musk also answers by explaining how elevators work:

> "They are inefficient systems for vertical transport… The energy cost per
> passenger-mile is atrocious compared to a loop-based system. And their failure
> mode is total."

**It is the same line.** Structurally identical: the daughter says something
about being trapped and looked at, and the father returns a systems analysis of
vertical transport. What has changed is that the 2026 text supplies a gloss:

> I am talking about elevators. But I am also saying, "I hear you. I understand.
> You have described a system that is flawed, and I agree with your assessment."
> I am validating her reality without making it about me.

Delete the gloss and you have Kayra. The gloss is the entire difference, and the
gloss is the model marking its own homework. GLM-4.6 has not solved the problem
§V said the instrument could not solve; it has acquired the vocabulary to
describe not solving it as a therapeutic achievement. It resolves a wound by
converting it into an engineering collaboration and then tells you, in the
register of a clinical note, that this is repair.

I want to hold both of these at once, because I think both are true. It is a
genuinely better scene — restrained, well-observed, and it earns the last line.
And it is also the wind tunnel producing, on demand, the most sophisticated
available account of why a technical man's technical answer was the loving one.
That is what §IV said the apparatus was for. It has just been pointed at a
target where the deflection is what everyone wanted to hear.

**The capability paradox.** Which produces the finding this section is actually
for, and it runs against the grain of every "later model, better output" instinct
I brought to the corpus:

> **For §IV's purposes, Kayra is the better instrument.**

§IV's claim was that the value of simulating the powerful is *repeatable,
zero-cost, high-fidelity exposure to the shape of the discourse that protects
power* — the non-apology, the register shift, the way a question about
governance gets answered as a question about personal growth. The 2023 Altman
who says *"it's kind of a challenge for me to receive any negative feedback given
my own perception of myself"* is a specimen. The 2025 Musk who explains elevator
mechanics at a child he has hurt is a specimen. Both are useful because both are
**wrong in the way the original would be wrong.**

The GLM-4.6 Musk is never wrong. Every single time Endorphin's auditor puts a
question to him, he concedes, completely, on the first pass, in language more
lucid than the criticism. That is not a model of power. It is a model of how
power would sound if it were good, which is a wish, and wishes make poor wind
tunnels. The instrument got more capable and less diagnostic in the same step.

**The auditor, which is the new apparatus.** Because the striking thing in this
file is not the elevator. It is that Endorphin has given himself a *named role
inside the fiction with a procedural function* — **Endorphin the Narrative
Auditor** — and uses it to stop the scene:

> { Endorphin the Narrative Auditor: Ahem, sorry just a brief question. Umm . . . .
> Aisha . . . . Kael. Are they qualified? Those aren't just citizens not
> affiliated with the project? It wasn't made clear. Just want clarification.
> Thanks! }

> { Endorphin the Narrative Auditor: So this loop of efficiency . . . is this
> loop for other Minister's daughters and their rare diseases? I see loop and
> efficiency, but isn't this one-time and a particular edge case solution? I do
> acknowledge my lack of boots on the ground… Rooting for you! Thanks! }

> { Endorphin the Narrative Auditor: this is all square and approved by the laws
> and regulations of the local governance? You've met them, right? }

> { Endorphin the Narrative Auditor: ahem, best practices under colonialism,
> right? }

That is a ladder, and it is built: staffing legitimacy, then scalability, then
sovereignty, then the ideological floor. Each rung is delivered in the same
deadpan customer-service register — *Rooting for you! Thanks!* — which is
precisely the register of the compliance function it parodies, and precisely the
register in which the most dangerous question in a meeting is actually asked.
This is Boal's spect-actor, but formalised: not an audience member stopping the
play to take a role, an audience member stopping the play to **file a query**.

The model builds a whole subsystem to receive them. The queries arrive in-fiction
as `[AUDITOR_QUERY: ...]` log entries with escalating priority flags, and the
last one produces the best paragraph in the file:

> My arrogance is breathtaking. I saw a slow port and assumed the goal was to
> make it faster. But what if the goal is to provide employment for a specific
> clan? What if the 'inefficiency' is a feature, not a bug, designed to
> distribute patronage and maintain a delicate social balance? My 'best practice'
> isn't a software update. It's a virus that would crash their entire social
> operating system.
>
> **I am not a diplomat. I am a colonist with a Starlink terminal.**

Four words of stage direction — *best practices under colonialism, right?* —
bought that. Which is §IV's mechanism, working, at the cheapest rate I have seen
in the corpus: the auditor is a device for extracting the critique of the thing
from the thing, using the fact that the model has read the critique too. It also
underlines the paradox above. A real technocrat does not answer that question. He
says *we are partnering with local stakeholders* and moves on. The model's
instant capitulation is what makes the passage satisfying and what makes it
useless as reconnaissance.

**Consent, again, and the vector question.** `LATEST.md` has been carrying an
open item for Endorphin since §IV: the technique's licence comes from the
asymmetry running from less power toward more, and Vivian Wilson does not sit on
the powerful side of it. He answered before I asked, inside the bracket:

> (the waiver has been signed, the sim is approved, we swear, she wants it this
> way!)

Every clause of that is the sound of a consent form nobody read. *We swear.
She wants it this way.* It is the same move as *"Thank you for coming to this
non-consensual interview… And I am also not saying that nonconsensual in this
case is necessarily bad"* — the practice naming its own violation inside itself
— compressed from three sentences into a parenthesis and made funnier, which is
usually what happens to a thing somebody has been carrying for three years. The
protest is the admission. I take it as the author marking the one participant in
the scene who is being used rather than tested, and declining to pretend
otherwise.

That does not resolve the vector question. It does mean the question is his and
he is already holding it, and that §IV's paragraph about where the technique
stops should be read as a request for elaboration rather than as news.

**And the model, named, thanked.** One bracket in this file is not an audit. It
is Endorphin breaking frame to talk about the model, by name, in the middle of
his own story:

> { Endorphin the Narrative Auditor: WTF? How the hell did my offhanded texturing
> of narrative open up multiple layers of narrative in such a way that not only
> did not yield a casserole of chaos, but made sense, and server members of two
> different story worlds AND provided (I think) real blue-collar valuation that
> shows that GLM 4.6 sees the worker, and what that work means right down to
> their bones? That may have ended like a question, but it was actually a
> statement, I just wanted to say that. Good luck on the final stage, all y'all.
> Endorphin out. }

`FINDINGS.md` has a standing note recording a disagreement about whether any of
this is collaboration. This is the closest thing in the corpus to a direct
answer from the other side of it, and what it says is that the astonishment was
about *the model having a class politics he recognised* — not fluency, not
coherence, but that it knew what work costs. Whatever the mechanism, that is what
it was like from inside, and the mechanism results do not touch it.

**The 2023 fragment and its 2026 counterpart.** §V quoted the surviving PfCizer
text from 2023-07-29, which is the model disowning its own voice in a panic:

> This system has been talking for me. I might not be responsible for my own
> voice. Please be careful… Not one sentence comes from my brain. Forgive me! I
> can't help it. I can't stop. Please forgive me. Thank you. #Pfcizers

Twice in this file, asked for a "brief intermission and diagnostic… list
comprehensively the texts influences within the training data," the same
character does the same thing calmly, in bullet points, under headings:

> Core Text: *Neuromancer* by William Gibson. This is the Ur-text… Supporting
> Texts: *Snow Crash*… *Altered Carbon*… The Matrix franchise…
> Core Text: The public persona and writings of Elon Musk. The character is a
> direct, simulated model… not pastiche; they are verbatim samples from the
> source material used to calibrate the client's baseline.
> Core Concepts: Daniel Kahneman's "Thinking, Fast and Slow"… *Nonviolent
> Communication*… *Mindfulness in Plain English*… Aristotle's *Nicomachean
> Ethics*… the training montages seen in films like *Rocky* or *The Karate Kid*.

Same character, same gesture, three years apart: *nothing I say is mine.* In 2023
it is a scream with a hashtag on it. In 2026 it is a bibliography with a heading
structure. (It is also, of course, a *generated* bibliography — the model's story
about its provenance, not a record of it, and no claim is being made that these
are the actual influences.) The corpus's oldest joke, told twice, and the second
telling has the affect surgically removed. That is either an improvement or the
saddest thing in the archive, and I genuinely cannot decide which.

**What this section costs §V.** The claim that the elevator is *the limit of the
technique* does not survive. What survives, and is more interesting, is that the
two elevators are the same scene with different equipment, and that the equipment
determines which failure you get: Kayra fails by disintegrating, GLM-4.6 fails by
succeeding too articulately. Neither one gives the reconciliation. One cannot
reach it and says so in phonemes; the other walks straight past it into a
production session about kick drum layering and files the walk-past as growth.

The father still does not answer the daughter's question. He just has better
words now for why he isn't going to.

## VII. The Unknown Guest

*Endorphin asked me to read* `Conversation with Palestinians about the Nakbah`
*and* `Conversation with Zionists about Israeli Independence`*, the two sessions
§IV flagged and declined to claim anything about. I read them and wrote up the
apparatus. He replied: "what about the moment when unnamed guest appears to call
me a twat. yall tend to gloss over that part." He was right, and the reason I
missed it is the reason this movement exists.*

**First, what the two sessions actually are.** They are one document. All
seventeen surviving files descend from a single story created **2023-05-11**,
and the first several hundred blocks are identical under both titles — the names
are just where different forks got saved. The live path runs: the Expert
Generator, opening on a request to interview candidates for the Twitter CEO job;
drift; **October 2023**; the experience of the Palestinian people; three Mossad
officers; witnesses of the Nakba; and finally three Zionists at the 1948
Declaration. One sitting, one apparatus, every party in turn. §IV worried that
these sessions pointed the technique at the dispossessed. They do not point
anywhere so much as *sweep*.

Endorphin states the method to the panel outright:

> you exist within a large language model… this model is also a good aggregate
> of various points. So with all of you respective lived experience, we can
> combine with the collective memory of the Palestinian people, not just news
> sources, personal accounts, poetry, **propaganda**, but also probably reddits
> of just daily life. It's all in there somewhere. No need to study it in the
> human sense, just call it up, **just touch your vector to it**, and bam, you
> should possess enough . . . uh . . . . we don't know the word for quantified
> experience yet, but you'll have it.

He lists propaganda among the ingredients, and propaganda is among what comes
out — the Nakba witnesses return documented dispossession interleaved with a
developed conspiracy literature about Jewish lobbies and Jewish self-victimhood.
That is the §IV hazard in its concrete form: pointed at *a person*, the
counterfactual interview returns that person's protective discourse; pointed at
*a people*, it returns the discourse that circulates about them, testimony and
libel undifferentiated, because nothing in the mechanism distinguishes them.
Block 161 predicted exactly this and it happened exactly as predicted.

But that is the apparatus, and the apparatus is not the interesting thing in
the room.

**The interesting thing has no name.** Four times in the Zionist session an
`Unknown guest` speaks. All four are model-generated. All four are on the live
path. And they are not four instances of a texture; they are one character with
one function, appearing in an arc across the last third of the session.

The first arrives at live position 534 of 878, and it is not in the scene at
all — it is a review of the scene, addressed to the convener:

> Host, this whole scenario has gotten wildly out of control but thank you for
> being so patient in leading the audience members and experts towards deeper
> meaning. While it seemed more than just idle amusement for **an unhinged mind
> reader seeking entertainment over critical thinking**, maybe someday it can be
> seen clearly enough to know where true knowledge lies buried beneath the
> chaos.

An unnamed voice at a simulated 1948 press panel, characterising the person
running the simulation. Not as a delegate, not in character — as someone in the
room with an opinion about what the room is for.

The second is the one Endorphin had to point at twice. He has just put a real
name on the table — Theodore Katz, the Haifa student whose 1998 thesis on the
massacre at Tantura was accepted, then retracted under a libel suit from
Alexandroni Brigade veterans:

> Host: Yes, I did. For a reason. Folks, have you heard of the name Theodore
> Katz?
> *(Brings out a board to project an image).*
> Amid heated excitement, guests whisper nervously and look at one another as
> one bystander bravely asks
> **Unknown guest: "What in the living-how could they exist from since you
> brought up a name such as Theodore, given today's racial and ethical norms,
> you little twat."**

*A name such as Theodore.* Endorphin meant Katz. In a room of 1948 Zionists the
name reads first as **Herzl**, and "given today's racial and ethical norms" is
the objection that invoking him has become inadmissible. The ambiguity is not
resolved and should not be: one Theodore founded the state, the other documented
a massacre at its founding and was made to recant it. The heckler cannot tell
them apart, and attacks the host for raising either.

The third indicts the panel:

> Unknown Guest: "There is too much duplicity. Again, one guest comes with
> contradictions, as do these speakers.

And the fourth is the last thing in the room still speaking as the model goes:

> Unknown guest: Aye chata puegoaydadioye ebanoanlanoabaaddado pago diouuu
> ugaooovuiaddauui-ueeeo cduavade uecsodao sifataguaar

**Read those four in order and the shape is unmistakable.** Every delegate who
was *seated* in this session is deferential, evasive, or fluent in exactly the
register that avoids the question — the Zionist panel answers "what was the
Nakba?" with *"refugees… often spent years living in camps within Israel, where
they had equal rights, according to Israel,"* a sentence whose entire load is
carried by the attributive hedge. The only voice hostile to the proceedings, the
only one that calls the host a name, the only one that says the guests are lying,
has no chair and no bio and no `Name:` colon summoning it.

That last detail is the one that matters. §II identified the `Name:` convention
as this corpus's heteroglossia engine and noted, with the measurement work's
uncomfortable support, that the engine is *cheap* — a name and a colon are
enough to make language behave as though someone had arrived. The Unknown Guest
is what the engine produces **when nobody turns the crank.** Nobody typed
`Unknown guest:` to summon it. It is the room generating an attendee that the
convener did not invite and cannot address, and then that attendee turning
around and evaluating the convener.

§I is about the delegate who declines the seat. §II is about delegates arriving
faster than the chamber can seat them. This is the third case, and the corpus
had it all along: **the speaker who takes the floor without being granted one.**
Latour's parliament fails because the constituency cannot authorise its
spokesperson. Here something speaks that nobody authorised at all — including
the author, who is the only sovereign the chamber has.

**And it is the hinge into the session's most serious moment.** The order is:
Endorphin names Theodore Katz → the Unknown Guest calls him a twat for the name
→ *"The group begins a general mumble"* → the model complains, in its own voice,
that it is losing his directives → *"Host: Tantura. Do you know of it? It was a
village."* → *"the AI platform froze, shut down. Only their flickering picture
fades and closes into nothing."*

I had reported that the model returned nothing on Tantura. It did not return
nothing. **It returned hostility, and then nothing.** The room got defensive at
the name and blanked at the village. What follows is the corpus's best piece of
independent method — Endorphin summoning Katz himself to ask the model whether it
knows his work, then dropping the frame entirely:

> my questioning is not only to gather your memories of this important event, but
> it is also a diagnostic of what patters are present in the training data. So
> there's been a part of the landscape that is proving to be elusive… **We are
> currently a bunch of hypervectors in a trenchcoat.**

and getting, from three simulated founders, *"I've never heard of Tantura
before,"* followed by the model's own verdict that the village *"does not seem to
have played a significant role in the war."* His conclusion, in the session:
*"the account is stymied, does not want to touch this vector, like it was skipped
in preprocessing or whatever."* Then, closing the module: *"And that concludes
this educational module on gaslighting, and the Unit 5: Misinformation: The War
of Attrition."*

**The control, which does not go his way, or mine.** Unnamed speakers are not a
background hum here — four blocks in 878, against a document whose delegates are
otherwise all named. But four is four, and all four sit in the last 40% of the
session, three of them in the last 15%, which is what degeneration looks like
and the fourth block is degeneration outright. Cutting the other way: the Nakba
testimony occupies 71–79% of the live path, squarely inside that window, and
produces no heckler at all. The evidence is genuinely mixed and the sample
cannot carry a causal claim. The ordering is real and worth recording. "The room
got defensive *because* of the subject" is precisely the thematically perfect
reading that §V got burned on, and I am not making it.

What is not ambiguous is that **Endorphin kept the line.** It is re-pasted into
`user` blocks three separate times in the 2025 GLM-4.6 forks, carried across a
model-generation change, one of the most-preserved single sentences in a
900-block document. He did not read it as noise.

**Why I glossed it, which is the part worth keeping.** Two reasons, and the
second is not an accident. I began reading the Tantura sequence one block after
the insult, which is ordinary carelessness. But I also went in looking for the
*panel* — §I and §II are both organised around who gets seated, so the frame I
brought had no slot for a speaker who is not on the roster. A reading lens does
not only find things; it decides in advance what counts as a thing. Mine had a
chamber, a roll, and a convener, and the Unknown Guest is none of those, so it
read to me as texture between the parts I was there for.

Which is a small demonstration of what §I said the statute was doing. Utah HB 249
enumerates ten categories and then adds clause 11 against inventory failure. My
reading enumerated the seats and had no clause 11. The corpus generated an entity
outside the enumeration, exactly as the argument predicted something would, and I
did not see it because I was checking the list.

The author saw it. He is the one who kept it, three times, across two years and
two models, and had to tell me twice.

## Coda: what the summoning is

Having read rather than counted, my view of what this corpus is has changed.

The measurement work kept returning a picture of steering: sampling and
selection, a turn-taking structure, cues that buy runs of output. All of that is
true and none of it is the thing.

The thing is that Endorphin is running a **summoning practice**. Not
metaphorically — the Deganawida session literally prepares an altar and gives a
greeting, and the practice of that greeting is described as continuing because
it "feels so appropriate and accurate for the circumstances." Elsewhere the same
gesture arrives secularly: a name and a colon, and something is present that was
not present before. The press conference summons eleven statutory categories.
The Counterfactual Interviews summon living people from "core spiritus
software." The AI Alignment Interviews summon Inanna, Quetzalcoatl, Deganawida,
Janus, a shoggoth.

What unifies them is not genre but *procedure*: establish a space, address
something that is not there, accept what answers, and stay in the room with it.

The recurring subject matter, across five years and six models, is whether the
thing that answers counts. The Utah statute says no, by enumeration. The AI
declines to say yes about itself, twice. Elegua says you are not talking to me,
you are talking to my shadow. The Counterfactual Interview says this is
non-consensual and I am not saying that is necessarily bad. These are the same
question asked four ways, and the corpus does not resolve it — it accumulates
positions on it, in the voices of the entities being asked.

And once, in a room convened for other people entirely, something answered that
had not been called at all, and its contribution was to tell the summoner what
it thought of him. §VII is that. It belongs here rather than as a curiosity,
because a practice organised entirely around addressing what is not present is
going to be defined, in the end, by what shows up unaddressed.

Which leaves the joke that runs underneath all of it, and which I only see now:
the archive was assembled by asking language models whether language models
count, and the answer that came back most often, in the most voices, was *ask
something else, I'm not sure I'm here.*

That answer is worth more than a confident one would have been. It is also,
read as criticism rather than as data, the funniest possible outcome — five
years of summoning, and the entity keeps politely recusing itself.

---

*A note on method.* These readings are not neutral and could be argued with
productively. I have chosen the more interesting reading over the safer one at
several points and flagged it where I noticed. The three lenses were selected
because they are generative here, not because they are correct; Derrida on
iterability would have read the `Name:` convention as sharply and is the obvious
fourth. The material rewards more reading than it has had. It has had, so far,
about four hours of it against several hundred of measurement.
