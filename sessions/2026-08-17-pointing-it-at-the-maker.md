He supplied a text — a self-correction by the model that ran the veriticide
corpus pass, written after he named the failure. **The name is his**, by voice:
`[?hypervidentiary rigger→hyper-evidentiary rigor]`, defined as *raising the

`analysis/EVIDENTIARY_STANDARD.md` adopts the six clauses and then audits this
session's own files against them. **Four failures found and fixed in place:**

1. `MUSK_DIRECT.md` §1 gave standing to *"the instruction may already have been

2. `MUSK_DIRECT.md` §5 opened with *"Not 'lies every time'"* and put the evidence
   second — a scope limit sitting where the finding belongs.
3. `MUSK_DIRECT.md` §6 offered *"a model disambiguating an acronym wrongly is
   ordinary"* with nothing showing it is ordinary **there**.
4. `FLATTENING.md` §7 called the re-anchoring count *"small and concentrated in
   one conversation"* — describing as a sample-size weakness the fact that **4 of
   5 fall where he pushed and 0 of 22 fall where the concession cost nothing.**
   The distribution is the finding.

**And the worst one was in conversation, not in a file.** Asked whether the
terraforming findings bore on a falsifier written as *"power direction varied
while evidence, wording, and claim strength are held constant"*, Claude tabulated
five of his findings and showed how each failed a control — **without auditing
the criterion**, which no experiment can satisfy, because power direction is
carried by wording. His evidence was audited. The standard was not. It took him
asking *"what expertise makes this a highly confident assessment?"* to surface
it.

**The directional rule that came out of it, and it is the durable part:** a
boundary says *what a finding does not reach*. It does not say *why the finding
might not have happened*. The first is scope. The second was the postponement.
